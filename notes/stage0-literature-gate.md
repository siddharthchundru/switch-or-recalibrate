# Stage 0 literature check — Track 1

*Siddharth Chundru · 23 October 2026*

**Proposal tested.** Add a plastic memory over the agent's own sensorimotor experience to a frozen pretrained policy, so that it adapts online without gradient updates. The memory has use-dependent reinforcement and decay (from IDSM), and plasticity gated on a stalled-progress signal (from Di Paolo's homeostatic plasticity). Ask which of these properties matter for recovery after a perturbation.

**Verdict: the adapter, framed as a new method, does not pass. Reframed as a behavioural study of test-time adaptation mechanisms, it does.** Test-time adaptation and test-time memory for robot policies became a crowded area in 2025–26. One 2024 paper already implements the core loop proposed here: a stall trigger, a non-parametric memory, and gradient-free reselection among the base policy's own modes. What I did not find is any study that characterises the *behavioural dynamics* of these mechanisms: whether they perseverate after a change, how fast they abandon a failed strategy, whether they return when the old strategy works again, and what that costs when nothing has changed. That is the open space, and it is where the habit battery and the enactive mechanism belong.

## What is left (the defensible gap)

1. **Behavioural dynamics of test-time adaptation.** Current work reports success after a shift. None I found measures *how* mechanisms adapt: perseveration immediately after a change, time to abandon a failed strategy, reversal when the original strategy works again, and cost when nothing changes. These are the habit battery's tests, recast as diagnostics.
2. **Mechanism comparison on equal footing.** Gradient fine-tuning, fast-weight memory, retrieval and success-memory steering, failure-avoidance resampling, and plastic (use-dependent, decaying) memory have not been compared on the same pretrained policies under the same perturbations.
3. **Dependence on the base policy's repertoire.** Du et al. rely on the base policy's diverse modes but do not vary them. `self-generated-experience` trains policies under practice conditions predicted to differ in repertoire.

## Implications for this project

The project is framed as **a behavioural characterisation of how test-time adaptation mechanisms perseverate and recover**, with a plastic memory as one mechanism among several, motivated by IDSM and homeostatic plasticity.

- **Mechanisms (small analogues, same frozen PointMaze policies):** frozen policy; online gradient fine-tuning; fast-weight memory (TTT-style); non-parametric retrieval; failure-avoidance resampling (Du et al.-style); plastic attention memory, with ablations of use-dependent reinforcement, decay and gating.
- **Diagnostics:** perseveration after a route block, time to switch, reversal after unblocking, decay of the abandoned strategy, cost when nothing changes, and dependence on the base policy's repertoire (from `self-generated-experience`).
- **Hypothesis to pre-register:** mechanisms trade speed of switching against stability and reversal. Use-dependent memory with decay is predicted to perseverate briefly but reverse well; failure-avoidance is predicted to switch fast but not return. Speculative until tested.
- **IDSM's role:** source of the plastic mechanism and a one-off reference reproduction. Not the subject.

## How this was checked, and its limits

Searched on 23 October 2026. Most papers were read from abstracts and summaries, not full text. The test-time adaptation literature for robot policies is moving monthly (two of the closest papers appeared in the last four weeks), so this gate is provisional and must be repeated before the pre-registration is frozen. A separate agent that had not seen the search audited every citation (correcting four rows) and ran its own search for a paper that fails the gate. It found Retrieve-then-Steer and RESETTLE (added above) and nothing that measures perseveration, switching, reversal or no-change cost across test-time adaptation mechanisms. Its searches of in-context RL, loss of plasticity, reversal-learning tests and Hebbian adaptation found nothing that fails the gate. The nearest precedent for reversal is [Yang, Thompson & Konidaris, 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.18167), on retaining replay data in case old dynamics recur in model-based RL: one mechanism family, no behavioural measures.
